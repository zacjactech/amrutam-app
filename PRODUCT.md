# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

Two overlapping audiences share one app:

- **New users discovering Ayurveda** — health-conscious people exploring herbal wellness for the first time, looking for expert guidance and trusted products without the clinical feel of telemedicine.
- **Existing Amrutam customers** — people already buying Amrutam products who want to consolidate their health journey: consultations with Ayurvedic doctors, curated product recommendations, and a personal health record in one place.

Both use the app in personal wellness moments — scheduling a consultation, reordering supplements, reviewing health records — not in clinical or emergency contexts. The primary task on any given screen is progressing through the Ayurveda journey: consult → treat → track → shop.

## Product Purpose

Amrutam is a complete Ayurvedic ecosystem in a single mobile app. It replaces the fragmented experience of visiting separate platforms for consultations, buying herbal products, and tracking health records. Success means a user never needs to leave Amrutam to manage their Ayurvedic wellness — from first consultation to ongoing health tracking to product replenishment.

## Positioning

One app for the full Ayurveda journey: expert consultations, curated herbal products, and personal health records — the complete Ayurvedic ecosystem.

## Brand Personality

**Earthy, premium, serene.** The voice is grounded and knowledgeable without being clinical. Tone is calm and reassuring — like a trusted Ayurvedic practitioner, not a tech platform. The app should feel like a quiet, well-made space for wellness, not a marketplace shouting for attention.

## Anti-references

- **Generic telemedicine apps** (1mg, Practo) — clinical, cold, insurance-form aesthetics. Insurance-form layouts, sterile blue-white palettes, and checkbox-heavy UIs. Amrutam must never feel like a hospital waiting room translated to a screen.
- Avoid clinical color language (hospital blues, sterile whites), overly dense information layouts, and the "fill this form to see a doctor" mental model.

## Design Principles

1. **Rooted, not retro.** Ayurvedic tradition is the foundation, but the interface is unmistakably modern. Heritage shows through content and voice, not through skeuomorphic or ornamental design.

2. **One journey, not three apps.** Consultations, shop, and health records are facets of a single wellness journey. Navigation and transitions should make the user feel they're moving through one connected experience, not switching between separate products.

3. **Earn trust through calm.** Premium feel comes from restraint, not decoration. Every element should reduce anxiety — about health, about spending, about whether this is the right choice. Quiet confidence over loud persuasion.

4. **Respect the moment.** People open this app during personal wellness moments. The interface should be unhurried, clear, and supportive — never pushy, never confusing, never making health feel transactional.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard mobile accessibility baseline: screen reader support with proper semantic labels, adequate touch targets (minimum 44pt), meaningful contrast ratios, and support for system text scaling. Reduced motion respected where implemented. No formal WCAG level target yet, but the baseline ensures the app is usable by people relying on assistive technologies.
